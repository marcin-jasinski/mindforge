- id: S1
  severity: major
  confidence: 0.75
  location: src/main/java/dev/mindforge/infrastructure/parsing/PdfParser.java:65
  claim: Every `Do` occurrence of the same image XObject is decoded, re-encoded to PNG and hashed again, so a small authenticated upload can make the parser do unbounded CPU and memory work (denial of service for all tenants).
  evidence: `DrawnImageCollector.processOperator` adds the XObject to `images` once per `Do` operator, and the parse loop calls `extract(drawn, extractor)` once per list entry. Dedup happens only in `ImageExtractor.emit` via SHA-256 of the emitted bytes, which is after `image.getImage()` plus `ImageIO.write(..., "png", ...)` (non-DCT path) or after a full `ImageIO` decode in `accept(byte[], String)` (DCT path). Duplicates are not counted by `checkCount`, so the 100-image cap never stops the loop. Concrete input: one page whose content stream repeats `/Im1 Do` 100 000 times (a few hundred KB, under the 50 MB `UPLOAD_MAX_SIZE` in application.yml), with `/Im1` a 7000x7000 solid-colour Flate image (declared 49 MP, below `MAX_PIXELS` = 50 000 000; compresses to a few KB). Result: 100 000 decodes of a ~196 MB raster and 100 000 PNG encodes of it, inside the upload request; the upload is eventually accepted with one IMAGE block. The same holds across pages (the AC4 "logo on every page" case scales to a 1000-page PDF). D05 requires dedup "once per document" and a bounded per-document cost; the bound is not reached here because the expensive work runs before the dedup check.
  fix: Skip an XObject that was already processed in this parse before decoding it, e.g. keep a `Set<COSBase>` (identity of `image.getCOSObject()`) in `parse` and `continue` when `add` returns false — the SHA-256 dedup in `ImageExtractor` stays for byte-identical images in distinct objects. Optionally also stop the per-page loop early once more than `MAX_IMAGES_PER_DOCUMENT` distinct images are accepted (call `checkCount(images)` inside the loop) so a PDF with thousands of distinct images is rejected before all of them are decoded.
